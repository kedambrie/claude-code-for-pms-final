Source: rook-database, pings and callouts tables, 29 Jun to 6 Sep 2026 (1,696 pings). Release 4.2 shipped 12 Aug and changed two things together: more weight on proximity, and ping wait cut from 90 s to 60 s.

1. Acceptance rate and missed pings per week

Acceptance % (line, left axis)Missed pings (bars, right axis)
  • The change is a step at the release, not a gradual dip. Weekly pings stayed around 160-177, so demand did not fall.
  • Turned-down pings barely moved (24-43 a week). The increase is almost entirely missed pings.
  • The week of 10 Aug includes the release (Wed 12 Aug), so it mixes before and after.

2. Pings per week: four responders vs the other twelve

Vesper, Farlight, The Undertow, Meteor MiteOther 12 responders
  • The four fell from about 12-14 pings each a week to 1-5. The other twelve absorbed the volume.
  • The headline rate recovering to 73% is partly because the four left the denominator. Farlight had no pings in the week of 31 Aug.

3. Acceptance by responder, before and after 12 Aug

Before (29 Jun to 11 Aug)After (12 Aug to 6 Sep)
  • The four were normal before the release (69-81% acceptance), then fell to 18-29%, mostly from missed pings.
  • The other twelve slipped by about 3 to 20 points and stayed at 64-71%, consistent with the 60 s cut.

4. Tickets vs pings: when each one moves

Missed pings"Gone before I could answer" tickets"Phone quiet / nothing coming through" tickets
  • Missed pings and "gone" tickets break on the release week. "Quiet" tickets start a week later and peak in the week of 24 Aug, after acceptance had begun to recover.
  • Tickets classified by keywords in the subject line (gone: 13, quiet: 30 through 7 Sep). The 7 Sep partial week is not shown.

5. Day by day around the release: why the quiet tickets lag

Missed pings, all respondersPings to the four responders
  • Missed pings peak on 13 Aug. The four were still being pinged on 13-14 Aug (mostly missed), then their pings fall to 1-3 a day from 17 Aug.
  • Farlight's last ping was 14 Aug, then none until 19 Aug. Ticket 3060, filed 17 Aug, says "not gone off in 3 days".

6. Share of pings in the responder's home area, before and after

Before (29 Jun to 11 Aug)After (12 Aug to 6 Sep)
  • Overall, home-area pings fell from 83% to 61%. Every responder's share fell, and the busiest responders fell furthest.
  • Before 12 Aug, none of the 186 out-of-area pings was taken (831 of 831 taken pings were home-area). After, 86 of 241 were taken (36%).

7. Callouts by area: share pinged to out-of-area responders

BeforeAfter
  • Uptown, Old Town and Harborside are the home areas of Farlight, Vesper and The Undertow. Callouts there now go mostly to responders from elsewhere.
  • Eastgate stays low because The Gale also covers it and is being pinged more. Other areas rise modestly.
  • "Home area" is the responder's area in the responders table compared with the callout's area. It is a coarse stand-in for proximity: there is no travel-time data.
What this data cannot show.
  • Cause. The pings table has no response-time, routing-score or location column, and the 60 s cut and the proximity weighting shipped together. Why the four were missed first, and whether a missed ping lowering rank locks a responder out, are hypotheses for Wen Li.
  • Response time. There is no "responded at" timestamp. The 92 s to 62 s figure is the gap between a missed ping and the next ping on the same callout, which confirms the ping wait cut but is not time-to-accept.
  • Seasonality. The data starts 29 Jun, so there is no prior year to test "August is always soft".
  • The four responders' "after" figures rest on only 11-17 pings each over 3.7 weeks.
